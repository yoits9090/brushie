# CPU image-representation audit

This audit is scoped to the updated acceptance target: a 4096x4096 RGB input already in memory, end-to-end encode (analysis through byte-stream construction) under 2.5 s on the reference Apple Silicon CPU, with no GPU and no per-image gradient descent. Reported timings from papers are not treated as CPU evidence unless the paper reports the CPU and thread count.

## Conclusions from the audit

The papers validate three useful design principles: (1) spatially local primitives and tile bins make random access cheap, (2) coarse-to-fine allocation is a better fit for stylized content than uniform sampling, and (3) quantization/entropy coding must be designed together with the primitive layout. They also make the central constraint clear: Image-GS's 3-4K local optimization steps, GaussianImage's 50K steps, and network-assisted methods cannot be honestly described as sub-2.5-second CPU encoders at 4096².

The prototype in this repository therefore uses a fixed analytical multiscale basis (5/3 lifting, equivalent to compact-support spline/wavelet atoms), a deterministic magnitude/structure gate, per-tile progressive streams, and Rice-style entropy coding. This is not claimed as a new wavelet transform; novelty risk is explicitly recorded in `design_candidates.md`.
